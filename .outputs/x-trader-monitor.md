For the market, this is a mixed signal. On the one hand, the data allows expectations to shift slightly toward a more dovish Fed policy. On the other hand, inflation is still not low enough for the market to begin pricing in aggressive rate cuts.

Therefore, the reaction across assets is also mixed.

$DXY may come under pressure due to softer rate expectations, but high annual inflation limits its downside potential.

$XAU is finding support from the slowdown in monthly inflation, but lingering inflation risks and real yields haven't gone anywhere yet.

The NASDAQ may react positively to easing rate pressure, but a more convincing decline in inflation is needed for a full-fledged revaluation.

$SPX is generally benefiting from reduced risk of a new inflation shock, but there is still no signal here of imminent large-scale easing of financial conditions.

Moving forward, I would closely monitor Fed comments, the labor market, and the PCE.

The next important event is the Fed meeting on September 15–16. That is when it will become clearer to what extent today's CPI has actually changed market expectations.
